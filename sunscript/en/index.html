How it works

Three lines, six directions.

Where on the screen you swipe doesn’t matter. Neither does length or speed. What counts is the direction and what your finger does along the way.

Blue: almost vertical. Up is a, down is i.
Red: shallow, like “/”. Up-right is e, down-left is o.
Green: slanted, like “\”. Up-left is u, down-right is y.
Six gestures

Swipe and let go: a vowel. The rest: consonants.

Here are all six on the blue line, upward. The other lines work the same way.

Space and delete

Tap: space. Double tap: delete a letter. Tap, then swipe left and hold: deletes letter by letter while you hold. Tap, then rest: a command, then a swipe. ↑ capital, ↓ Enter, ↗ symbols, ↘ language.

With two hands

Whichever finger touches first is the first beat. Tap, then a line: pause. A line, then a tap: hold. Two lines the same way: next. Opposite ways: back.

Game

First repeat. Then recall.

Each level adds one gesture: first you repeat it with a hint, then you recall it without one. Stars only for what you recalled yourself. At the end, an exam and a 60-second sprint; records stay on this device.
